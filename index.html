Origins of Imagination

Photos: Dominic Harris Studio

This is a popular digital art installation at the Naturalis Biodiversity Center in Leiden and sees hundreds of visitors daily.

It consists of a large LED video wall with an accompanying LiDAR sensor to detect and emulate touch. Visitors draw a butterfly on a piece of paper, scan it, and watch it fly into the canvas and become a permanent member of the collection. It has become a collaborative project between us and all of the museum's visitors.

My work at the digital art studio necessitated shared tooling for a multitude of large scale interactive and immersive installations.

Necessary features:

  • An understanding of the space and the people within it in real-time
  • A method to emulate touch on large arbitrary surfaces (like in Origins of Imagination)

Huge emphasis is placed here on performance. The computational-time budget is very small, countable milliseconds.

Robotics

I've worked on projects involving kinetic installations. One such project, is a piece that used special chromatic disks to reflect static lights around a room. The disks' movements scatter light on and around people in the space.

Components:

  • Multiple disks. We used 7 and 13 because of the possible arrangements. Each disk was controlled by two motors, themselves controlled by a PI.
  • RGBD cameras. Used to map the space and people in it in real-time
  • An orchestrator to coordinate all the movement
